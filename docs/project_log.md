## Remaining Limitations (updated 2026-07-17)

- Both engines have two verified W1-W5 correctness runs, W1-W5 latency sweeps (10/20/40 records/sec plus a 20/sec repeat), and a 30-minute sustained-load stability check on W1-W4 plus a bounded check on W5, all with calibrated T0-T3 decomposition.
- A resource-utilization sampler exists for both engines, but the two engines' collection windows do not match (short bursts for Kafka Streams, one blended multi-workload average for Flink); this needs reconciling before the figures are a controlled comparison rather than an observation.
- A full fault-injection matrix (jvm_kill, broker_kill, node_loss) exists for both engines on W1 identity only; no stateful workload has been fault-injected yet, which is the single experiment most likely to change this paper's conclusions about recovery cost.
- A tuning matrix exists for both engines: one alternate commit/checkpoint interval value, on one workload (W3). No broader search space, and no memory-pressure, storage-tier, or partition/parallelism sweep from the original proposal exists.
- Every fault-injection and tuning-comparison cell is a single trial; none have confidence intervals or repeated-trial variance. This is the paper's most consistently named weakness and the rubric's own single-run gatekeeper would discount these findings accordingly if applied strictly.
- No RocksDB, changelog, or checkpoint-storage-specific metrics are collected (only container-level CPU/memory/network via `docker stats`).
- No infrastructure-cost attribution or throughput envelope exists; the paper cannot yet fully answer any of the three proposed research questions, only bounded pieces of each.
